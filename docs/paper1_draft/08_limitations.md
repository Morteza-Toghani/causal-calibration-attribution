# 8. Limitations

**Number of seeds and the permutation test.** We used 10 training seeds for Hopper and 5 for Walker2d. The 95% $t$-intervals assume approximately normal seed-level effects, which we could not verify at these sample sizes. The exact paired sign-flip test has $2^n$ equally likely assignments, so the smallest attainable two-sided $p$ is 0.00195 at $n = 10$ and 0.0625 at $n = 5$. No Walker2d condition can therefore reach $p < 0.05$ in this test, even before multiplicity correction, and the Walker2d evidence rests on effect sizes and confidence intervals. The Hopper seeds also come from two groups, five from the legacy pipeline and five retrained later, and we did not test whether the groups differ.

**Single model class.** We evaluated one model family, a 5-member Gaussian MLP ensemble with a fixed architecture and fixed hyperparameters. We did not test transformers, random-feature models, other ensemble sizes, or other uncertainty methods, and we make no claim that the ranking of mechanisms holds for them.

**Two locomotion environments.** We used Hopper and Walker2d from the Minari medium datasets. We did not test manipulation, pixel-based, or other domains, other dataset qualities, or other simulators. The two environments agree in the sign of every recalibrated effect but differ in magnitude by a factor of up to about 2.5 for observation effects (0.20125 vs 0.07955), and two environments are too few to explain this difference.

**Severities are not matched across mechanisms.** The noise fractions, mass scales, and action scales are not expressed in a common unit, and we tested two levels per mechanism. The ranking of mechanisms is therefore a ranking of effects at the chosen settings. A different choice, for example a larger action change, could change the ordering of dynamics and policy effects, and could change the size of the gap to observation shift. We did not estimate dose–response curves.

**Simulator-specific bias.** The re-simulation bias we found has a mechanism specific to MuJoCo: `set_state` followed by `step` does not reset `qacc_warmstart`, and the observation does not contain the x-position. Other simulators may show similar biases from different causes. The correction relies on the identity-intervention baseline cancelling the bias in the paired difference. We verified this through the null control but did not test whether the bias interacts with the intervention, that is, whether the bias at mass scale 0.80 equals the bias at scale 1.0.

**Recalibration is minimal.** We used a single variance scaling factor per seed, fitted at the nominal 0.90 level. The recalibrated baseline is not perfectly calibrated at other levels. More expressive recalibration, such as isotonic regression or per-dimension scaling, may change the effect sizes, and we left it for future work. The recalibration factor was fitted on a calibration split that is itself subject to the same sampling variability across seeds.

**Metric limitations.** The primary metric is unsigned and instance-level. It does not show the direction of miscalibration, which is why we report signed coverage, and its values are not directly comparable with macro-averaged calibration errors in other work (Section 4.6). The negative dynamics effects reflect a decrease in the deviation from nominal coverage and should not be read as an improvement in predictive quality. We did not analyze the secondary metrics here, and so we cannot say whether they agree with the primary metric.

**One-step prediction only.** All evaluations concern one-step prediction at fixed states. We did not test multi-step rollouts, where errors compound and the policy interacts with the model, and we did not test downstream planning or safety outcomes.

**Intervention isolation.** We did not measure cross-effects of the interventions, for example whether the mass change alters the observation distribution of the reconstructed states, or whether the action change moves states away from the data distribution. The interventions were applied one at a time and we did not test interactions between mechanisms.

**Causal language.** Causal statements are limited to the controlled computational design: an analyst-assigned intervention on a fixed trained model in a simulator, with matched instances. We make no claim about causal identification in real systems. The explanations offered in the Discussion for why recalibration matters and why `observation_high` changes sign are hypotheses that we did not test directly.
